# Vibration severity zones — general guidance

## Overview

Vibration on rotating machinery is usually judged against three broad zones. These zones help an operator decide whether a machine can keep running, needs attention soon, or must stop immediately.

## Zone A — good

- Vibration is low and stable.
- No action is needed.
- Trend monitoring is sufficient.

## Zone B — acceptable

- Vibration is higher than new-machine levels but still safe for continuous operation.
- Start looking for causes such as small imbalance, mounting looseness or process changes.
- Schedule a non-urgent inspection.

## Zone C — alert

- Vibration has crossed a sustained threshold and may damage the machine or hurt product quality.
- Take action within days: spectrum analysis, visual inspection and root-cause check.
- Do not leave the machine unattended without a plan.

## Zone D — danger

- Vibration is high enough to risk a near-term failure.
- Shut down or reduce load as soon as safely possible.
- Inspect before returning to service.

## Notes for CNC spindles

- Spindles run at high RPM, so small amplitude changes at high frequency matter more than the same amplitude would on a slow pump or fan.
- Always compare X and Y together; a fault that affects only one direction usually points to a coupling, belt or mounting issue.
